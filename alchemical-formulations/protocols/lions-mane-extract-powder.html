Research & sources

Lions Mane (Hericium erinaceus) has drawn a growing body of clinical and preclinical research, particularly around nerve growth factor and cognitive function. A selection of published studies is linked below for anyone who wants to dig deeper — presented as further reading, not as claims about this specific product.

  • Mild cognitive impairment, double-blind placebo-controlled clinical trial in 50–80 year olds — PubMed
  • Reduction of depression and anxiety after 4 weeks' intake, randomised controlled trial — PubMed
  • Neurotrophic properties and nerve growth factor (NGF) induction, laboratory study — PubMed
  • Peripheral nerve regeneration following crush injury, animal study — PubMed
  • Neuroregenerative potential in peripheral nerve injury, review — PubMed

These are links to independently published research on Lions Mane generally — not studies of this specific product, and not medical advice. See "What we don't claim" below.

What we don't claim

This describes traditional and historical use, not a medical claim — Lions Mane is a dietary supplement, not a licensed medicine. It's not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease, and isn't a substitute for a varied, balanced diet or professional medical advice. Don't exceed the recommended intake, and speak to a doctor first if you're pregnant, nursing, taking medication, or managing a health condition. Keep out of reach of children.
