The same pearl powder as our full-size 200g bag, in a smaller starter size — a good way to try it, or to keep a travel-sized supply on hand. Pearl powder has been valued across traditional systems for centuries — not only for its beauty, but for its relationship with refinement, balance and long-term nourishment. In Traditional Chinese Medicine it is known as Zhen Zhu Mu, and was famously used daily by the Empress Dowager Cixi of the Qing Dynasty, associated with calming, grounding and cooling qualities during periods of stress or depletion.

Research & sources

Pearl powder and nacre (mother of pearl) have drawn a small but genuine body of published research into their mineral composition and physiological effects. A selection of studies is linked below for anyone who wants to dig deeper — presented as further reading, not as claims about this specific product.

  • Pearl powder and sleep disturbance stress response, rat model of sleep deprivation — PubMed
  • Water-soluble pearl powder components and wound healing, laboratory study — PMC
  • Nacre (mother of pearl) and stimulation of bone formation, in vivo and in vitro study — PubMed
  • Powdered nacre and visceral fat reduction, animal study — PubMed
  • Efficacy of protein-rich pearl powder on antioxidant status, randomised placebo-controlled trial — ScienceDirect

These are links to independently published research on pearl powder and nacre generally — not studies of this specific product, and not medical advice. See "What we don't claim" below.
